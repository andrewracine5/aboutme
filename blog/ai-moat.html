Your AI Stack Isn't a Moat. Here's What Is.

AR
Andrew Racine
Marketing leader focused on demand gen and growth.

If everyone uses the same models on the same data, everyone reaches the same conclusions. That's the trap a lot of demand gen teams are walking into right now.

I got a version of this question in my Sharebird AMA on AI and demand generation: if every team has access to the same AI tools, where's the moat five years out? It's a question I keep coming back to, so I wanted to give it more room than an AMA answer allows.

Why AI Can't Be the Moat

Every week there's a new agent, a new workflow, a new way to produce ten versions of an ad in the time it used to take to write one. That's real, and teams that ignore it will fall behind. But not falling behind and getting ahead are different problems.

Hamilton Helmer has a useful test for any advantage. It needs a benefit, what you gain, and a barrier, why rivals can't copy it. AI tools pass the first test easily. They fail the second one completely. Anything you can buy, your competitor can buy next quarter.

Rory Sutherland makes the same point from a different angle. If everyone uses the same data and the same logic, everyone reaches the same answer. AI makes the logic cheaper and faster. It also makes it more uniform. When every team's first draft comes from the same handful of models, the first drafts start to look alike.

Simply spend 15 minutes on LinkedIn these days and you will be amazed how many posts and comments all follow a similar pattern and tone. It is so interesting that people and companies would risk ruining their own brand in the name of shipping content more easily.

So AI is a short-term advantage. Product and strategy you can buy. Culture and brand you have to earn.

Where the Moat Actually Is

Here's where I'd bet. Each of these is hard to copy, and each one takes time.

  • Brand and mental availability. Only about 5% of accounts are in market at any moment. When one of the other 95% starts looking, more and more of them will ask an AI assistant for a shortlist. The brands that show up are the ones the market already knows and talks about. That compounds over years, and there's no shortcut.
  • Proprietary data and insight. Your first-party data, your customer conversations and your own research. Original benchmarks and tools rivals can't replicate are true assets. Everyone else is working from the same public internet.
  • Trust and relationships. Community, events, customer advocates and executive relationships. These are costly signals, and costly signals create trust because they're costly. They get more valuable as everything else gets cheaper to produce.
  • A distinct point of view. When information is commoditized, personality and perspective are what make people pay attention. Different beats better.
  • Your team's judgment and speed of learning. Two companies with identical tools will get very different results depending on who's using them and how fast they learn.
  • Distribution you own. An audience, a newsletter, a community. Rented attention gets more expensive and more crowded every year.
The tools change every six months. The psychology of buyers hasn't changed in a hundred years.

What I'd Do About It Now

Knowing where the moat is doesn't help much unless it changes what you do on Monday. A few things I'd act on.

Split the job in two. Brand keeps you remembered by the 95%. Demand capture finds and times the 5%. AI is a huge help on the second job. It's a weak substitute for the first.

Protect the unmeasurable. Brand, events and high-effort ABM will always look bad in last-touch attribution. Cutting them is how you lose next year's pipeline.

Automate the plumbing, keep humans on the signals. Let AI research an account deeply, then have a person write the message that matters. A handwritten note or a custom teardown works because someone clearly spent time on it. Strip out the effort and you strip out the meaning.

Build one asset nobody else has. A benchmark, an assessment, a piece of research built on your own data. It will outlast a lot of AI-generated content that reads like everyone else's.

Start owning a channel. Pick the audience you want and start building the newsletter or the community now. It's slow, and that's the point. Slow is what makes it hard to copy.

The Irony

AI pushes demand gen back toward fundamentals. Psychology, brand, story and relationships were always the moat. AI just removes everything else that used to differentiate.

Use the tools. Get fluent, fast. But don't confuse the tools with the advantage. The teams that win five years from now won't be the ones with the best stack. They'll be the ones buyers already know, trust and remember.
